§A. THE TWO OPEN SURVIVORS
================================================================================

Each is (a) a corpus gap with a NAMED record, (b) an argument that does not
quite close, or (c) the search's own negative control. None is left as "no
record distinguishes it".


1. `4f89b00b`   `list_read_ints:288`   `p >= len` -> `p > len`
   (b) AN ARGUMENT THAT DOES NOT QUITE CLOSE, and the hole is named.

   `p == len` at the top of the item loop happens only on the first iteration
   and only when the leading scan consumed all 200 bytes, i.e. every byte of
   `Words(1)` is a blank or an end-of-line character.

   IF THEY ARE ALL BLANKS the mutant is equivalent: `GetWords` fills words in
   order, so a blank `Words(1)` means the line had NO words at all and
   `Words(2)` is blank too, hence `rec[200]` is a blank; the mutant then falls
   through to `parse_int` at `p == len`, finds no digits, and returns 5010 where
   the original returns -1. The reference reads only `/= 0`.

   THE HOLE: `is_eol` is also accepted by that scan. A `Words(1)` made entirely
   of CR or LF bytes is a WORD (neither is in `GetWords`' separator set), so
   `Words(2)` may then be a real key -- and if that key begins with `'/'` the
   mutant returns 0 where the original returns -1, which the unit sees.

   That record needs a CR or LF byte inside `FileLines`, and R6's character
   corpus is "the reference's own character literals and their collating
   neighbours", which contains none. So it is not declarable and not currently
   reachable.


2. `e8cf2d25`   `list_read_ints:261`   `is_eol(rec[p])` -> `is_eol(rec[p + 1])`
   (a) CORPUS GAP with the SAME cause as 1, and they are ONE gap rather than
   two.

   The edit is on the SECOND disjunct of the leading scan,
   `while (p < len && (is_blank(rec[p]) || is_eol(rec[p])))`. `||` short-circuits,
   so the mutated operand is evaluated only where `is_blank(rec[p])` is FALSE --
   that is, at the character the scan STOPS on. It differs only if some
   `rec[p + 1]` at that stopping character is a CR or an LF.

   No byte of any record in any corpus this campaign has generated is a CR or an
   LF, so the two programs cannot be told apart.

   (The RUNBOOK's own `||`-short-circuits rule, one operand over: unit #55
   measured five record forms killing every `is_blank(rec[p + 1])` and leaving
   every `is_eol(rec[p + 1])` alive, for exactly this reason.)


   BOTH OF THEM, AND THE DECISION THEY NEED
   ----------------------------------------

   `list_read_ints` accepts CR and LF as blanks. That is RIGHT and REACHABLE in
   unit #55, whose record is the 2048-byte `Line` and can hold a line
   terminator. It is unreachable here, whose record is a `GetWords` word.

   The campaign's usual answer to a branch no input can take is to DELETE it and
   write the proof (unit #32's `LEN_TRIM`, unit #43's `cols > 0` guard, unit
   #55's `negate_cond 9381bdef`). **That answer is not available**, because the
   reader is a byte-for-byte P4 copy of unit #55's -- `check_p4_blocks.py`
   hash-verifies 3,855 bytes of it -- and deleting the branch would dissolve the
   shared measurement the two units' readers rest on (113 records through
   gfortran's own READ, which is what makes either of them checkable).

   THREE OPTIONS, and the choice belongs to the Driver rather than to this unit:

     * add `CHAR(10)` / `CHAR(13)` to R6's character corpus -- which touches
       every CHARACTER-input unit in the campaign and would re-price a lot more
       than these two mutants;
     * accept them as a permanent property of the shared reader and declare them
       with that reason -- which needs a judgement kind this campaign does not
       have, because it is neither "the programs agree" nor "the corpus never
       runs the line";
     * split the reader so each unit carries only the separator set its record
       can contain -- at the cost of the P4 relationship that makes the two
       units checkable against one another.

   Raised in DECISIONS.md with its general form: **a P4 copy inherits its
   source's REACHABILITY as well as its bytes**, and this is the first time in
   this campaign that the copy relationship has BLOCKED a repair rather than
   transmitted a defect.


§B. WHY THE SEVEN THAT DIED, DIED -- the control on three corpus changes
================================================================================

The first sweep, on the 11,863-case corpus, killed 89 of 143 with 54 survivors.
The record search named FIVE of them as corpus gaps with an ADMISSIBLE record.
Three R14 entries over two rounds closed them, and the arithmetic at every step
is a prediction rather than an explanation afterwards:

  ROUND 1, loop 343f843, seven `_NEIGHBOUR_VALUES` entries (the INTEGER type's
  limits as SINGLE WORDS, a null repeat at count one and at count three, and a
  value followed by one non-terminator):

    08ce3b56  :351  `k = 0` -> `k = 1`        HEAD|1*|            0 vs -1
    17de888e  :363  `rec[p]` -> `rec[p + 1]`  HEAD|0+|         5010 vs 0
    9e4b76b0  :240  `<` -> `<=`               WORDADM|w:imin|     0 vs 5010
    cb35366f  :240  `>` -> `>=`               FULLADM|full:intmax| 0 vs 5010

    PREDICTED four kills and nothing else.   MEASURED 89 -> 93, and the
    survivor set differs by exactly those four ids.

  ROUND 2, loop 581925d, three entries in three different blocks, for the three
  survivors left that the search had named:

    3545d5d7  :342  `count <= 0` -> `count < 0`   record `0*/`     KILLED
    5002a20d  :81   `200` -> `201`                a 201-byte lead  KILLED
    ab7420d6  :332  `q < len` -> `q <= len`       a `*` neighbour  SURVIVED

    PREDICTED three kills.   MEASURED 93 -> 95.   ONE PREDICTION FAILED.

  **AND THE FAILURE IS THE FINDING.** The form written for `ab7420d6` was a
  full-width lead carrying a repeat count of its own,
  `"0"*189 + "3*123456789"`, paired with a `'*'` neighbour. It cannot reach the
  bound it was aimed at: **a lookahead that scans for a token STOPS AT that
  token**, so the `*` the record carried is exactly what kept the digit scan
  from running to the record's last byte. The scan reaches `len` only on a
  record with NO star in it, and the mutant that reads one past the end then
  finds its star in the NEIGHBOUR -- and the digit run must ALSO be a legal
  count, which leading zeros give for free.

  ROUND 3, loop 8f9d72f, the same entry corrected to
  `("digstar", lambda n: "0"*(n-1) + "1", "*7")`:

    ab7420d6  KILLED.   MEASURED 95 -> 96, and nothing else moved.

Three predictions, two exact and one refuted, and the refuted one produced a
rule about record design that neither the earlier units nor this one's first
attempt had: it is now in the RUNBOOK and in `generate.py`'s own comment.

§C. WHAT 0.9796 DOES NOT COVER
================================================================================

1. **The mutants the operator cap never enumerated.** `cppmutate` offers 40 per
   operator and this translation's `compare_op` and `const_tweak` are both
   capped (`capped_operators` in the artifact). Their survival is UNKNOWN, not
   none (P6). `scripts/mutation_cap_audit.py` prints the table. The cap is not
   raised here: every other unit in this campaign was scored at it, and changing
   a verification default mid-run is X3.

2. **The echo record on unit `UnEc`.** Not translated -- a Fortran unit number
   cannot cross the boundary -- and MEASURED DEAD on this unit's own lines:
   `ROSCO_Helpers.f90:186` runs 1,680 times across the 27 scenarios and `:187`
   never fires. Closing it needs a Fortran shim that owns the unit, not a corpus
   change. Unit #54's family decision; this is the FOURTH of the five units
   carrying the identical statement.

3. **`is_eol` in the copied reader.** Reachable in unit #55, where the record is
   the 2048-byte `Line`; not reachable here, where the record is a `GetWords`
   word. Two open survivors live there (§A.3 and §A.4) and the honest repair is
   a decision about the shared reader rather than about this unit.

4. **The comma and semicolon branches of the reader**: 22 never-executed lines
   carrying all 8 `unreachable` declarations, at three sites --

       :300  x3   the blank scan inside the `rec[p] == ','` null-value branch
       :311  x2   the semicolon's `first_item || sep != Sep::Blank` test
       :316  x3   the blank scan inside the semicolon branch

   Grouped by REGION as the RUNBOOK asks, they share ONE cause: `GetWords`
   splits on `,` and `;`, so no input to this unit can put either character in
   the record. That is a property of the PROGRAM rather than of the corpus, so
   NO corpus change empties the group -- which makes this the case the RUNBOOK's
   "an `unreachable` set with one common cause is a corpus gap wearing a
   declaration's name" does NOT cover, and saying so is the point of grouping
   them.

   Four more mutants on those same lines are declared EQUIVALENT instead
   (`10e4743a`, `6713e5fa`, `715eaff6`, `ed5b251a`), because an equivalence
   survives a corpus that later reaches the line and `make_unreachable.py` skips
   any mutant that already carries one.

   The `is_eol` lines inside the same region (`:272` and its `crossed_eol`
   consumers) carry no surviving mutant, but they are never executed for the
   OTHER reason -- the corpus has no CR or LF byte -- and that is the same gap
   as item 3.
