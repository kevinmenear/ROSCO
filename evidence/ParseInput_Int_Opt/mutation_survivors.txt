§A. THE TWO THAT WERE OPEN, AND THE ONE BYTE THEY BOTH TURNED ON
================================================================================

The first dispatch closed at 96 / 98 = 0.9796 with two survivors, and recorded
them as ONE gap with three options none of which was a unit's to take. The gap
was real. The reading of it was wrong in a way that is worth writing down,
because the wrong reading is what made it look like somebody else's decision.

    4f89b00b   `list_read_ints:288`   `p >= len` -> `p > len`
    e8cf2d25   `list_read_ints:261`   `is_eol(rec[p])` -> `is_eol(rec[p + 1])`

BOTH ARE KILLED. `mutation/ParseInput_Int_Opt.json` survivors: [].

WHAT THEY NEEDED, at the level of the one character that does the work
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

`e8cf2d25` sits on the SECOND disjunct of the leading scan,
`while (p < len && (is_blank(rec[p]) || is_eol(rec[p])))`. `||` short-circuits,
so the mutated operand is evaluated only where `is_blank(rec[p])` is false --
at the character the scan STOPS on. Every record in every corpus this campaign
had generated begins with a digit, a sign or a letter and is followed by
another such, so both spellings read "not an end-of-line" and agreed.

  R14 `("eollead", "\r7")` -- a CR then a digit. The original crosses the CR and
  reads 7 with status 0; the mutant, testing the DIGIT for end-of-line, does not
  enter the scan at all, meets the CR in the item loop, and `parse_int` finds no
  digits: 5010, `aviFAIL = -1` and a message. The unit sees all three.

`4f89b00b` sits at the top of the item loop. `p == len` there happens only when
the leading scan consumed all 200 bytes -- every byte a blank or an end-of-line.
A record of all blanks is NOT A WORD, so no word splitter produces one; that is
why no corpus and no record search had ever reached the line.

  R14 `("eolfull", lambda n: "\r" * n, "/7")` -- 200 bytes of CR, with `/7` as
  word two so record byte 201 is a `/`. The original returns END (-1); the
  mutant reads byte 200 and finds the list-directed terminator, returning 0 with
  the item untouched. THE NEIGHBOUR IS THE DESIGN: with any other byte there
  both spellings end non-zero and a caller that reads only `IOSTAT /= 0` cannot
  tell them apart -- the same shape as round 2's `repzeroslash`.

CR and LF both, for the reason `_NUMERIC_LEADS` carries `6E1`, `7D1` and `8Q1`
rather than one of them: `is_eol` is a disjunction, and a mutant that drops only
one disjunct needs the other byte in the corpus to die.

WHAT THE FIRST DISPATCH GOT WRONG, and it was not the arithmetic
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

It recorded `is_eol` as "right and REACHABLE in unit #55, whose record is the
2048-byte `Line`, and unreachable here, whose record is a `GetWords` word", and
raised the general shape as **a P4 copy inheriting its source's REACHABILITY as
well as its bytes** -- the first time the copy relationship had BLOCKED a repair.
On that reading the only repairs were to widen R6's character corpus for the
whole campaign, to invent a new judgement kind, or to split the shared reader.

THE ASYMMETRY DOES NOT EXIST. Unit #55's `Line` comes from the same
`FileLines` this unit's `Words` comes from, and
`evidence/ParseInput_Int_Opt/eol_admissibility_probe.txt` bounds both the same
way: libgfortran treats a bare CR as a RECORD TERMINATOR and strips a CR that
precedes the LF, so five written lines come back as SEVEN records and no
end-of-line byte can enter `FileLines` through
`ReadControlParameterFileSub`'s `READ (u,'(A)')` at all.

So `is_eol` is not in the reader because unit #55's record could hold a line
terminator. It is there because **gfortran's INTERNAL list-directed READ treats
CR and LF as separators** -- measured over four records by unit #55
(`evidence/ParseInAry_Opt/parser_conformance.f90:73-75,148`) and over six more
by this unit (`record_form_probe.txt`, forms `eolhead` .. `eolslash`, 47 of 47
agreeing on (IOSTAT, value)). That is a property of the RUNTIME both units
transcribe, not of where their bytes came from, and it is why the repair is an
ordinary corpus widening rather than a decision about the shared reader.

AND THE BOUNDARY THAT REMAINS, stated rather than buried
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------

The record is admissible to the UNIT and not producible by its shipped FILE
READER, and those are two different questions:

  * `FileLines` is `CHARACTER(*), INTENT(IN), DIMENSION(:)` and CHAR(13) is a
    value that dummy holds; `GetWords` splits on `' ,!;''"'//Tab`
    (ROSCO_Helpers.f90:145) and neither byte is in that set, so a run containing
    one is copied into `Words(1)` whole. That is the campaign's admissibility
    bar -- the same bar that makes a 200-digit word or `nan(aaa...)` a legal
    corpus record though no `DISCON.IN` contains one either.
  * No `DISCON.IN` can deliver one through the READ. Measured.

There is ONE shipped path the second bullet does not bound, and it is named
because it is the honest edge: `ReadControlParameterFileSub` counts lines by
reading until IOSTAT goes non-zero, so `NumLines` is one MORE than the file has;
it ALLOCATEs that many and reads that many, and the last READ fails.
`FileLines(NumLines)` is therefore never assigned, and `FindLine` loops over
`1, SIZE(FileLines)` including it. What that element holds is undefined, and
undefined is not "blank". It is not offered as the justification -- the
dummy-argument domain is -- but a claim that no shipped input can contain the
byte would be false, and this is why.


§B. WHY EACH KILL HAPPENED -- the control on four corpus rounds
================================================================================

The first sweep, on the 11,863-case corpus, killed 89 of 143 with 54 survivors.
The record search named FIVE of them as corpus gaps with an ADMISSIBLE record.
Four rounds of R14 entries closed every one, and the arithmetic at every step is
a PREDICTION rather than an explanation afterwards.

  ROUND 1, loop 343f843, seven `_NEIGHBOUR_VALUES` entries (the INTEGER type's
  limits as SINGLE WORDS, a null repeat at count one and at count three, and a
  value followed by one non-terminator):

    08ce3b56  :351  `k = 0` -> `k = 1`        HEAD|1*|            0 vs -1
    17de888e  :363  `rec[p]` -> `rec[p + 1]`  HEAD|0+|         5010 vs 0
    9e4b76b0  :240  `<` -> `<=`               WORDADM|w:imin|     0 vs 5010
    cb35366f  :240  `>` -> `>=`               FULLADM|full:intmax| 0 vs 5010

    PREDICTED four kills and nothing else.   MEASURED 89 -> 93, and the
    survivor set differs by exactly those four ids.

  ROUND 2, loop 581925d, three entries in three different blocks, for the three
  survivors left that the search had named:

    3545d5d7  :342  `count <= 0` -> `count < 0`   record `0*/`     KILLED

    ab7420d6  :332  `q < len` -> `q <= len`       a `*` neighbour  SURVIVED

    PREDICTED three kills.   MEASURED 93 -> 95.   ONE PREDICTION FAILED.

  **AND THE FAILURE IS THE FINDING.** The form written for `ab7420d6` was a
  full-width lead carrying a repeat count of its own,
  `"0"*189 + "3*123456789"`, paired with a `'*'` neighbour. It cannot reach the
  bound it was aimed at: **a lookahead that scans for a token STOPS AT that
  token**, so the `*` the record carried is exactly what kept the digit scan
  from running to the record's last byte. The scan reaches `len` only on a
  record with NO star in it, and the mutant that reads one past the end then
  finds its star in the NEIGHBOUR -- and the digit run must ALSO be a legal
  count, which leading zeros give for free.

  ROUND 3, loop 8f9d72f, the same entry corrected to
  `("digstar", lambda n: "0"*(n-1) + "1", "*7")`:

    ab7420d6  KILLED.   MEASURED 95 -> 96, and nothing else moved.

  ROUND 4, loop 8fa5440 (plus the `_cesc` repair in b3ad414), four entries --
  `("eolfull", "\r"*n, "/7")`, `("lffull", "\n"*n, "/7")` in
  `_NEIGHBOUR_TAILS` and `("eollead", "\r7")`, `("lflead", "\n7")` in

    4f89b00b  :288  `p >= len` -> `p > len`               KILLED

  THE CONTROL THAT HAD SOMETHING AT STAKE IN ROUND 4. `26bfb57b` (`:261`,

§C. WHAT 1.0000 DOES NOT COVER
================================================================================

1. **The mutants the operator cap never enumerated.** `cppmutate` offers 40 per
   operator and this translation's `compare_op` and `const_tweak` are both
   capped (`capped_operators` in the artifact). Their survival is UNKNOWN, not
   none (P6). `scripts/mutation_cap_audit.py` prints the table. The cap is not
   raised here: every other unit in this campaign was scored at it, and changing
   a verification default mid-run is X3.

2. **The echo record on unit `UnEc`.** Not translated -- a Fortran unit number
   cannot cross the boundary -- and MEASURED DEAD on this unit's own lines:
   `ROSCO_Helpers.f90:186` runs 1,680 times across the 27 scenarios and `:187`
   never fires. Closing it needs a Fortran shim that owns the unit, not a corpus
   change. Unit #54's family decision; this is the FOURTH of the five units
   carrying the identical statement.

3. **The comma and semicolon branches of the reader**: 22 never-executed lines
   carrying all 8 `unreachable` declarations, at three sites --

       :300  x3   the blank scan inside the `rec[p] == ','` null-value branch
       :311  x2   the semicolon's `first_item || sep != Sep::Blank` test
       :316  x3   the blank scan inside the semicolon branch

   Grouped by REGION as the RUNBOOK asks, they share ONE cause: `GetWords`
   splits on `,` and `;`, so no input to this unit can put either character in
   the record. That is a property of the PROGRAM rather than of the corpus, so
   NO corpus change empties the group -- which makes this the case the RUNBOOK's
   "an `unreachable` set with one common cause is a corpus gap wearing a
   declaration's name" does NOT cover, and saying so is the point of grouping
   them.

   Four more mutants on those same lines are declared EQUIVALENT instead
   (`10e4743a`, `6713e5fa`, `715eaff6`, `ed5b251a`), because an equivalence
   survives a corpus that later reaches the line and `make_unreachable.py` skips
   any mutant that already carries one.

   **THIS ITEM WAS THREE ITEMS AT THE FIRST DISPATCH AND IS NOW ONE.** The
   `is_eol` lines inside the same region (`:272` and its `crossed_eol`
   consumers) were listed beside it as a second, different gap. They are no
   longer a gap: the corpus contains CR and LF records and the two mutants that
   lived there are killed. The lines that remain uncovered are the comma and
   semicolon ones alone, and their cause is the program.

4. **The 37 equivalences are claims, and the space they are measured over is
   now stated with its own limit rather than with a silent one.**
